# Cooling the city: the case for street trees

A evidence review for a city resilience committee. The source compares heat exposure, tree cover, costs, and implementation time.

<div class="rule"></div>
## Why cities overheat

<p>Dark roofs, asphalt, and low tree cover combine to raise surface and air temperatures. The effect is uneven: neighbourhoods with less shade can be several degrees hotter than leafy districts nearby. The committee asked for a practical comparison of interventions, not a general essay on climate change.

## What the evidence says

<ul>
<li>Mature street trees cut peak street temperature by up to 4.2°C.</li>
<li>One mature tree can intercept about 1,400 litres of rainfall a year, reducing pressure on drains.</li>
<li>Tree cover in the surveyed district fell from 29% to 21% over ten years.</li>
<li>Residents in planted streets report fewer heat-illness days after two summers.</li>
<li>Maintenance costs are lower than installing and running new air conditioning.</li>
</ul>

## Implementation sequence

<p>The committee’s preferred programme runs in four stages: survey streets and heat exposure; plant in winter; water and maintain for two years; evaluate shade, rain interception, and resident health. The order matters because planting without maintenance produces little shade and wastes the establishment cost.

<div class="callout"><strong>Recommendation.</strong> Treat shade as infrastructure. Fund a ten-year planting and maintenance programme instead of buying short-lived cooling equipment.</div>
## Limits

<p>Trees take time to mature. They are not a substitute for emergency cooling during extreme heat, and species selection must account for water use, roots, and local climate. The evidence supports a mixed programme, not a single intervention.

<div class="page-break"></div>
## What to measure

<p>Track canopy cover, peak street temperature, rainfall interception, maintenance cost per tree, and heat-related health visits. A dashboard should show the trend over time, not only a single year’s result. The strongest story is the relationship between planting, shade, and health—not just the number of trees planted.

<div class="quote">“The cheapest cooling is a tree that was planted ten years ago.”</div>
